This is the real interface running in your browser, just without the sound. Have a go with the knobs and presets.

Why I built this

A friend of mine built a guitar amp and sent it over as a VST3 plugin. I wanted to play through it properly, alongside the NAM models and cab IRs I was already using, and I didn’t want to buy a whole amp-sim app just to try one amp.

I’ve paid for a few of those apps before. The cheap ones run out of features fast, and the expensive ones are fiddly and stuffed with things I’m never going to touch. So Rigbench is small: a plugin, an amp, and a cab, each with an on/off switch.

What it does

  • Loads a VST3 plugin, such as an amp, a drive, or an effect, and opens the plugin’s own window when you need it.
  • Loads NAM amp models (.nam files). If your interface runs at a different sample rate, Rigbench resamples as you play, so you don’t have to care.
  • Loads a cab IR (WAV, AIFF, or FLAC) with no added latency.
  • Runs them in that order: plugin, then amp, then cab. You can switch any slot off. The amp has a level knob and the cab has a mix knob.
  • Has a tuner, and a noise gate on the input to cut the hiss between notes with high-gain models. The gate is off until you turn it up.
  • Saves the whole rig as a preset: what’s in each slot, every knob, and the plugin’s own settings.
  • Shows your audio device, sample rate, buffer size, and round-trip latency along the top, and the signal on a scope as you play.

Under the hood it’s a C++ audio engine built on JUCE. The interface runs in a webview on its own thread, so moving knobs and drawing the scope don’t add latency. The source and build steps are on GitHub.

What you need

  • A guitar and an audio interface. The first time you open Rigbench, it asks you to play a few notes and finds which input your guitar is on.
  • A NAM amp model, a cab IR, or both. There are free ones under Where to get sounds.
  • A VST3 plugin, if you have one you want to play through. You can leave that slot empty.

This is the setup screen. In the browser it pretends you have a two-input interface with the guitar on input 2.

On a Mac, macOS won’t open Rigbench the first time, because it’s self-signed rather than notarised by Apple. Try to open it once, then go to System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. You only have to do that once. Then allow microphone access, which is how macOS lets it hear your guitar.

On Windows it needs the WebView2 runtime, which current versions of Windows 10 and 11 already have. The first time, SmartScreen may warn you because the app isn’t signed: click More info, then Run anyway.

Plugins, and presets other people share, run code inside the app, so only load ones you trust.

It’s early days: this is v0.1.0. If something doesn’t work, or there’s something you’d like it to do, open an issue on GitHub.

Where to get sounds

Plenty of amp models and cab IRs are free. The plugin slot is optional.

  • For amp models, TONE3000 is where most people share their NAM captures. Downloads are free; you need an account. The NAM subreddit is good for recommendations.
  • TONE3000 has cab IRs too. If you just want one to start with, Jester Dyne’s Emerald pack is a Marshall 4x12 with Greenbacks, free with no sign-up.
  • For the plugin slot, these are free, open source, and run on macOS, Windows, and Linux: ChowDSP’s BYOD for drives and pedals, Airwindows Consolidated, and Dragonfly Reverb. The openaudio list has plenty more.
